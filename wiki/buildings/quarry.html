Quarry

Costs24 planks
Building work300 work units, the material paid in 2 stages; up to 3 builders at once — about 5 min for one builder with a hammer
Posts1 worker
ToolPickaxe, one per worker
Makes1 stone every 60 s
Fetched once it holds4 stones — a default, changed on the buildings tab
Needsthe ground may vary by up to 2 blocks over the site; at least 100 blocks of natural stone to work
VersionsStandard, Cross gable
Level 2works at 150 % pace
Upgrade to 212 planks, 12 ashlars, 2 fittings; 300 work units in 2 stages; once 8 people work and live within 48 blocks of it and it has run 1 day without a problem

The quarry breaks stone out of a pit beside its shed — the rubble your stonemason squares into ashlar. It takes something out of the ground instead of off it, and the ground stays changed.

  • Its pit is half of the building. The shed stands on the side the road comes from, the pit lies behind it, and the placement preview shows both. It only goes where there is rock under the pit: at least 100 blocks of natural stone within ten layers under the floor. If there is not, the preview says "There is too little stone under the pit here" and names the amount. Look for a hillside or a rocky plain rather than a meadow on deep earth.
  • It digs layer by layer. Every piece of work takes one block out of the pit — always from the highest layer left, so the pit sinks evenly instead of as a shaft.
  • The pit is wider at the bottom than at the top, but never reaches past the building's own ground. A ladder at the shed grows with it, so you can climb down and back out at any time; the quarryman works down there too.
  • Only stone counts. Stone, granite, diorite, andesite, tuff and deepslate are broken out; ore is left standing, so a vein of iron in your pit is still there for you. Grass, earth, sand and gravel lying over the rock are cleared away with the first block taken out of their column — they yield nothing, they are just out of the way.
  • Water and lava are walled off. What stands against a fresh face as a liquid is replaced by rock, so a pit that reaches an aquifer does not drown itself or the quarryman in it.
  • A pit runs out. Ten layers deep is the whole of it. When nothing is left, the quarry stops and says so ("has no Rubble left within its reach"), and that is final: nothing grows back. Tear it down and build a new one on fresh rock. The hole stays when you do — a quarry changes the land for good.
  • The stonemason uses the rubble. Without one, rubble lies in the store. The quarry stops by itself once 64 rubble are lying ready — one more for every stonemason — and starts again when there is room for more. Without a stonemason, that also means a pit with more stone than that in it is never worked out.

Level 2

At level 2 the quarry works at 150 % pace: every block of stone takes two thirds of the time. The rules of every upgrade are on Upgrades; the cost and the neighbourhood it needs are in the table at the top of this page.

  • You can tell it by stone and lanterns: its walls are masoned, and lanterns hang in place of torches. At the Cross gable the basket of the loading beam hangs on an iron chain instead of a rope of wood.
