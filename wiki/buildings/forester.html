The forester plants trees within its reach, so that the forest your lumberjacks fell grows back. It needs nothing delivered: a sapling is not a good of the settlement, and the forester plants as long as a spot in its reach is free, whether a lumberjack stands nearby or not. When none is left it says “… has no free planting spot left within its work radius.” It plants twice as fast as one lumberjack fells, so beside one hut the wood grows until no spot is left free.

  • Trees grow on the settlement's time, about half a Minecraft day after the forester plants them, whatever vanilla would do. Vanilla may still grow one of its own saplings earlier; that is harmless.
  • The forester plants scattered, not in rows, and keeps clear of walls, of roads and of an orchard's plantation.

Which trees it plants

The forester plants the trees of the biome the spot lies in — spruce in the taiga, birch in a birch forest, acacia in the savanna, cherry in a cherry grove, small jungle trees in the jungle. Many biomes mix in a second kind, a forest one birch to four oaks. Which kind a spot gets depends on the spot alone: the same spot always gets the same kind.

  • The biome is read at the spot, not at the hut. A forester on a biome border plants differently on either side of it.
  • Wood is wood. Every kind of tree brings the same raw wood, at the same pace.
  • Dark oak, pale oak and mangrove are never planted, because they do not grow from a single sapling on plain ground. In the dark forest the forester plants oak and birch, in the pale garden birch, in a mangrove swamp oak.
  • A biome of another mod gets the trees of its kind as long as that mod files it under the usual biome tags; a biome the table does not reach gets oak.
  • The orchard plants oaks wherever it stands, because the apples hang on them.

Level 2

At level 2 the forester works at 150 % pace: planting a sapling takes 80 seconds instead of 120. The rules of every upgrade are on Upgrades; the cost and the neighbourhood it needs are in the table at the top of this page.

  • You can tell it by stone and lanterns: its walls are masoned, and lanterns hang in place of torches.
