Mine

Costs32 planks
Building work360 work units, the material paid in 2 stages; up to 3 builders at once — about 6 min for one builder with a hammer
Posts1 worker
ToolPickaxe, one per worker
Makes1 iron ore every 4 min
Makes1 fuel every 3 min
Fetched once it holds4 iron ores, 4 fuel — a default, changed on the buildings tab
Needsthe ground may vary by up to 2 blocks over the site; at least 64 blocks of ore in its band
VersionsStandard, Shaft house
Level 2works at 150 % pace
Upgrade to 216 planks, 12 ashlars, 2 fittings; 360 work units in 2 stages; once 8 people work and live within 48 blocks of it and it has run 1 day without a problem

The mine sinks a shaft and works under the ground, for iron ore and for coal. It needs ore in the rock beneath it, so it cannot stand just anywhere. On a hillside, the adit mine does the same work into the slope.

Finding a spot

  • Choosing a mine lays an ore map over your claim. Every chunk is coloured by how much ore of one seam a mine in its middle would find: red is none, orange too little for a mine, yellow enough, light green at least twice that and green at least four times. Grey means the area is not loaded — walk there and choose the mine again. Each field has a line in its own colour along its edge; under a shader pack the fields turn pale, so go by the lines. Press Z to switch between coal and iron; a line over the hotbar names the seam shown and the key. It is the key left of X, which a German keyboard labels Y.
  • The placement preview gives the exact numbers for the spot you are aiming at, one line per seam — 70 blocks of coal in the rock below, 4 blocks of iron ore in the rock below. A spot needs at least 64 blocks of ore, coal and iron together; with less the box is red and says too little ore in the rock here. You never have to dig to find out what is underground.
  • Read both numbers before you build. Coal is the common seam: a spot can be green on coal alone and hold no iron at all. If it is iron you are after, walk on until the iron line is worth the shaft.
  • The band it works is 6 to 30 layers under its floor, 10 blocks around the shaft. Ore outside the band is not counted and never dug.
  • The mining areas of two mines never overlap, and an adit mine counts as a mine here. The mining area is the ground a mine works, seen from above: the circle of 10 blocks around the shaft. How deep the band lies makes no difference. Where two areas would share ground the box is red and says Its mining area overlaps that of another mine — move the mine further off. The preview does not draw the areas.
  • A road joins it like any other building. Its road side lies on the edge of its plot, in front of the drive up to the shaft.

What it digs

  • It works two seams in the proportion you set: iron ore out of an iron vein, fuel out of a coal seam. The share of each is a lever on the politics tab, 50 to 50 to begin with. A seam the settlement has enough of, or that is worked out, is skipped, and the other one takes the work. So a mine on coal is a second source of fuel beside the charcoal pile.
  • The ore goes to the smelter, and the fuel to everything that burns it — hearths, toolmaker, bakery, brewery, smelter and smithy. A mine on its own fills its shelf and stops, saying the settlement has enough; build the smelter and it goes on.

The workings

  • It really digs, and you can walk through the workings. A shaft with a ladder sinks from the mouth in the yard, and galleries three blocks wide and three high run from it to the ore, propped with pit timber and lit by torches. Take the ladder down and walk in; nothing down there is dark.
  • The galleries lie on levels, five blocks apart. For each seam the mine works the level nearest the surface that still holds that ore, and sinks the shaft deeper only when it has to.
  • The timber and the torches cost you nothing. They come out of no store, so a mine props and lights its galleries even when the settlement has no wood.
  • The miner works underground, at the end of the gallery being cut, or at the foot of the shaft while it is being sunk. Between two shifts they climb back out. If you want to watch them, follow the torches.
  • What it does not mine, it leaves standing. Other ore the gallery passes stays in the wall, and water and lava are walled off as the gallery is cut, so the workings do not flood.

When a seam runs out

  • The message says which seam is gone — “has no Iron Ore left within its reach, and it does not dig beyond that” — and the mine goes on with the other one. So the message does not mean the whole mine is finished: a mine with only coal left may simply be resting because the settlement has fuel enough.
  • A seam set to 0 % is never dug. The mine says “Mine 1 digs no Iron Ore: its share is 0 %.” until you raise the share.
  • Nothing grows back. Once both seams are gone, tear the mine down and sink a new one on fresh ore.

Level 2

At level 2 the mine works at 150 % pace: a block of iron ore takes 160 seconds instead of 240. The rules of every upgrade are on Upgrades; the cost and the neighbourhood it needs are in the table at the top of this page.

  • It goes on working the same shaft; the upgrade leaves the workings alone.
  • A worked-out seam keeps it from rising, like any other standstill.
  • You can tell it by stone and lanterns: the walls are masoned, lanterns hang in place of torches, and a lantern on a chain hangs over the shaft.
