Watchtower

Costs56 planks, 16 torches
Building work700 work units, the material paid in 4 stages; up to 4 builders at once — about 11 min 40 s for one builder with a hammer
Posts1 soldier
Beds1
Stands byits soldiers fight for every watchtower, charter and village gate within 96 blocks, anchor to anchor
Land9×9 chunks around the chunk it stands in; at least 3 chunks from the charter and every other tower
Needsthe ground may vary by up to 3 blocks over the site
VersionsStandard, Open lookout
Level 22 soldiers; 2 beds
Upgrade to 228 planks, 16 ashlars, 4 fittings; 700 work units in 4 stages; once 8 people work and live within 48 blocks of it and it has run 1 day without a problem

A watchtower pushes the boundary out once its first guard takes the post. From then on it claims 9×9 chunks around the chunk it stands in; the charter holds 7×7. A site claims nothing, and neither does a finished tower whose post nobody has taken yet — it says so with the hint “Watchtower 2 waits for its first guard: until one takes the post, it claims no land and cannot be attacked.” A tower has to be built inside your land like any building, so one at the edge moves the boundary up to 64 blocks further out, and a second one on the new land moves it again. While you place it, the outline of the whole square stands around the model.

A watchtower costs torches as well as planks, and torches need fuel. Your toolmaker makes them out of fuel, and the way to fuel is a charcoal pile, which costs nothing but planks. Until a toolmaker is placed, the watchtower stays grey on the build tab. In short: no fuel, no new land.

You may build right up to a neighbour or a village — the square simply stops there. It takes nothing that already belongs to another freehold, and it keeps one chunk clear around every chunk of a village that has a bed in it. If a neighbour later gives land up, your towers take in what they had to leave out. Towers keep their distance: a tower has to stand at least 3 chunks from your charter and from every other tower of yours. When the first guard takes the post, the chat says how many chunks you gained — “Watchtower 2 is manned: Oakhill now claims 32 more chunks.” —, a yellow line shows the new square for ten seconds, the march stones move out to the new edge, and the villages near the new boundary are found — see Villages.

Once manned, a watchtower holds its land for as long as it stands, also when the guard leaves the post later. Only tearing the tower down gives the land up, and that is refused while a building, a site or a road of yours stands on ground that would go with it — the message says how many of each, and they have to go first. Ground that the charter or another tower also covers never goes.

A watchtower's guard is a soldier, and the tower is where raiders come. The nearest tower that has had a guard is the target of a raid on your settlement, and its guard fights first. The guards of every tower within reach of a target — the number is in the table above — fight after those of the target, whether the target is another tower, the charter or a village gate. A guard carries the gear the settlement brings them — see Soldiers and gear.

A watchtower can be conquered. The soldiers of another settlement may attack it once it has had a guard; if they win, the tower is theirs, with the land only it held, and whatever of yours stood on that land is torn down with nothing returned. A tower keeps one guard when it sends soldiers out on an attack of its own, so a tower of level 1 sends none.

  • A fallen guard's post is filled from a barracks first. If a barracks has the tower within its reach, one of its soldiers moves up, with their gear and their wounds; otherwise the settlement fills the post like any other.
  • Standing guard counts as work. The guard keeps their post even with nothing to do. After dark the guard walks a round of the tower with a torch; by day they are at their post inside.

Level 2

At level 2 the watchtower has two guards and two beds instead of one. It rises like any building with a post — an empty post or a message holds it back; the rules are on Upgrades, the cost and the neighbourhood it needs in the table at the top of this page. Its land stays the same: a tower of level 2 holds as many chunks as one of level 1.

  • You can tell it by stone and lanterns: the guard room is masoned all round under a shaft of timber, lanterns hang instead of torches, and the two beds stand one behind the other at the east wall.
