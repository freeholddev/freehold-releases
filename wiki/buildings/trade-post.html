Trade Post

Costs24 planks, 12 ashlars
Building work360 work units, the material paid in 2 stages; up to 2 builders at once — about 6 min for one builder with a hammer
Posts1 worker
Tradeswith the nearest connected village whose gate lies within 96 blocks as the crow flies and is reached over finished roads
A triptwice the way to the gate and 4 min more; up to 16 units of goods, at least one whole lot
A village sells4 loaves for 1 emerald, 4 fish for 1 emerald, 6 fuel for 1 emerald, 1 iron ingot for 1 emerald, 4 iron ores for 1 emerald, 8 wheat for 1 emerald
Needsthe ground may vary by up to 2 blocks over the site
VersionsStandard

The trade post trades with a village. Its one worker, the trader, walks over your roads to the gate of a connected village, sells what the settlement has to spare and buys what you have set a target for, and pays in emeralds. It is the only way emeralds come into a settlement and the only thing that spends them.

Who it trades with

A trade post trades with the nearest connected village it can reach. The village has to be connected to your settlement, its gate has to lie within reach of the post in a straight line — the number is in the table above — and a finished road of yours has to lead from the post to that gate. If several qualify, the nearest one is the partner. Without one it stands still and says why:

  • “X has nobody to trade with: no village is connected to the settlement.” — connect one with a village gate.
  • “X has nobody to trade with: no connected village lies within its reach.” — build a post nearer to the gate.
  • “X has nobody to trade with: no road leads from it to the gate of a connected village.” — draw one.
  • “X has nobody to trade with: its village is recovering from a lost raid and trades with nobody for a few days.” — a waiting message; the trade comes back by itself, see Raids.

A trip

A trip takes twice the way to the gate and a fixed time on top. The trader loads whole lots — as many units as the village pays an emerald for — up to a load of 16 units, but always at least one lot, so 32 logs for one emerald go along too.

  • At the end of the trip the trader sells first and buys after. The emeralds and what was bought go into the post's own pile and are carried to the store like any output; whatever did not sell goes back into the pile it came from.
  • A village only trades so much a day. It turns over one emerald a day for every bed it has, shared by every trade post that trades with it — see the charter's page for the number.
  • Every emerald traded raises the reputation of your settlement with that village, and a high reputation brings more people from it — see Villages.
  • If the connection is lost while the trader is out, everything comes back as it went: no sale, no purchase, no reputation.

Selling

The post sells only what the settlement has to spare. On the buildings tab its stock lines are what it sells — one for every good a village pays for, starting at 0. Raise a line, and the carriers bring the post that much of the good, but only out of surplus: stock above the good's ceiling that no site and no upgrade has set aside.

  • A sale target counts in whole lots. A lot is what a village takes for the price — 16 planks, 8 ashlars — and a click on - or + moves the target one lot, Shift ten. The line says “Planks: 0 of 16 (in lots of 16)”.
  • The sale target makes the settlement produce that surplus. A good is made until the store holds its ceiling plus the sale targets of all trade posts for it: two posts with a target of 16 planks raise the limit by 32.
  • What each good fetches is in the last column of All goods; a good without a price there is not taken.

Buying

The post buys up to a target you set. Under Buys up to: on the buildings tab there is one line for every good the village sells, starting at 0. The trader buys while the settlement holds less of the good than the target and emeralds lie ready, and the post orders those emeralds from the store by itself. What a village sells, and for how much, is in the table at the top of this page.

What you see

  • The trader walks the trip: over your roads to the gate, down the link road into the village to its bell, and back the same way — out with the goods in hand, home with an emerald. The rest of the time they stand behind the counter of the post.
  • A post that does not set out says what it waits for: “X has nothing to sell yet: not one lot of Y lies in its store.” Waiting between two trips is normal.
  • The buildings tab says how it stands: “Trades with a village of N beds: reputation R, E of M emeralds traded today.”, while the trader is out “The trader is back in 4:00 min.”, and “N emeralds for purchases lie ready.” Without a partner it says “Trades with nobody: …” and the reason.
