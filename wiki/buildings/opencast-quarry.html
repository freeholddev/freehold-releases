Opencast Quarry

Costs24 planks
Building work300 work units, the material paid in 2 stages; up to 3 builders at once — about 5 min for one builder with a hammer
Posts1 worker
ToolPickaxe, one per worker
Makes1 stone every 60 s
Fetched once it holds4 stones — a default, changed on the buildings tab
Needsthe ground may vary by up to 2 blocks over the site, and rise towards its working side up to 6 blocks above its floor; 24 blocks from the next building of its trade; the ground 10 blocks behind its working side at least 3 blocks higher; at least 40 blocks of natural stone to work
VersionsStandard, Lean-to roof
Level 2works at 150 % pace
Upgrade to 212 planks, 12 ashlars, 2 fittings; 300 work units in 2 stages; once 8 people work and live within 48 blocks of it and it has run 1 day without a problem

The quarry for a hillside. Where it may stand is explained under Building against a hillside.

  • The three rows at the back of its plan are the floor of the cut, and from them the work carries ten rows further into the hill. The preview shows the rows; the hill behind them is what gets taken.
  • It works into the slope as a staircase, in benches three layers high, starting at the bottom right behind its yard. The stepped face moves away from the yard into the hill as the work goes on, and the quarryman stands on the step in front of it.
  • Grass and earth over a block of stone go with it, with no yield, as at the quarry. Ore stays standing.
  • It needs 40 blocks of natural stone in the cut before it may be set, counted the same way the pit is counted. It reaches twelve layers above its floor and two below.
  • It gives the same rubble as the quarry, at the same pace, and it stops at the same point when enough is lying ready. Which of the two you build is a question about the ground, not about the yield.
  • Two opencast quarries keep 24 blocks apart, so two cuts never eat into one another. An opencast quarry and an ordinary quarry keep no distance — their workings may overlap.

Level 2

At level 2 the opencast quarry works at 150 % pace: every block of stone takes two thirds of the time. The rules of every upgrade are on Upgrades; the cost and the neighbourhood it needs are in the table at the top of this page.

  • You can tell it by stone and lanterns: its walls are masoned, and lanterns hang in place of torches.
