The toolmaker makes the settlement's own gear out of planks: the axe, hoe, pickaxe, shovel and fishing rod, the saw, hammer, chisel and bellows, and a soldier's spear and shield — carts as well, with fittings from the smithy, and torches with fuel. Which tool is needed by which post is on Tools.

It shares its planks and fittings with your building sites. When there are not enough to go round, the sites together get their share of the loads and the toolmaker the rest, so a long build queue does not leave it without planks — see Who gets a scarce good.

The toolmaker makes one thing that is not a tool: the torch. One plank and one unit of fuel give four torches, and a watchtower is built with sixteen of them.

Torches come as soon as a building site needs them, ahead of any tool that nobody is waiting for. A carrier often takes them from the toolmaker straight to the site, so a store without torches does not mean none are being made; how many a site has is on its line on the buildings tab.

Without fuel it makes no torches. Once it has nothing else to make, and nothing in the settlement produces coal or charcoal, a hint over the roof points at building what does. Your people carry on making tools as before, and the hint goes once fuel arrives from a charcoal pile or a mine.

It equips the soldiers too. The settlement keeps a spear and a shield ready for every soldier's post and one more of each, so the toolmaker makes both even before the first tower stands — see Soldiers and gear.

The toolmaker stops when the settlement has enough. It makes one tool for every post that needs it — a hammer for every builder's post too — and two spares of each tool. Once those are lying ready it says "has stopped: the settlement has enough" and names the tool. That is not a problem: as tools wear out or as you build another hut, the work comes back by itself.

See also: Tools.

Level 2

At level 2 the toolmaker has a second post: two toolmakers work at once, each at a bench of their own, where on level 1 one worker goes from the first place to the second. The rules of every upgrade are on Upgrades; the cost and the neighbourhood it needs are in the table at the top of this page.

  • Its upgrade costs planks and fittings, which it also works with. The carriers bring the goods for the upgrade and the goods for its work separately, and neither eats into the other.
  • You can tell it by stone and lanterns: its walls are masoned, and lanterns hang in place of torches.
