Tavern

Costs32 planks, 12 ashlars, 2 fittings
Building work500 work units, the material paid in 3 stages; up to 3 builders at once — about 8 min 20 s for one builder with a hammer
Posts1 worker
Keeps in stock16 beer — a default, changed on the buildings tab
Reach24 blocks
Needsthe ground may vary by up to 2 blocks over the site
VersionsStandard, With Hall

The tavern pours the beer, and whoever lives in a house within its reach works a quarter faster — at every workshop and on every building site. It needs a keeper, one of your people, who stands behind the counter all day.

  • Its reach is 24 blocks, measured in a straight line between the tavern and the house, at any height. A house within the reach of two taverns belongs to the nearer one. Your charter and a watchtower count as houses.
  • It pours one beer a day for every person it serves — sixteen people, sixteen beer. A tavern with nobody in reach pours nothing and tells you so; that is a hint, not a fault, and it goes quiet when you put a house near it.
  • Faster means four fifths of the time. A job or a building stage started by a served person takes a fifth less; a job already running keeps the pace it started with. Carriers are not affected, and neither is the keeper, who has no job of their own. The buildings tab of the charter screen tells you on the job line: faster thanks to the tavern, for as long as that job runs — even if the tavern has run dry since it started.
  • Its light shows whether it pours, the way a house's does: the torches burn while the keeper is there, the fireplace only while there is beer. A lit tavern with a cold fireplace has run dry.
  • It is the last post to be filled, unless you move it up on the politics tab — see Where a free resident goes. Bread comes before beer: a settlement short of people staffs every workshop and even the watchtower first, and the counter last. Until a keeper comes, the tavern stays shut and dark.
