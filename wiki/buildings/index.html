Every workshop starts with one worker, and you grow by building another one. Two lumberjack huts match one sawmill exactly — so the second hut is how you double your wood. A building kept busy in a busy neighbourhood later rises to level 2 on its own: a workshop gets a second post, a building that works the land works half as fast again, a house gets two more beds and a storehouse more room.

More builders fill the first site, then start the next — see Builders' Yard.

The build tab lists the buildings under headings — General, Wood, Stone, Food, Iron and tools, Supply, Trade and defence — and shows a building grey while something it costs cannot reach the settlement: none of it in the store, not a basic good you may hand in, no building of yours placed that makes it, and no trade post that buys it. See The charter screen.

Building numbers

Every building carries a number among the buildings of its kind in its settlement, and is named with it wherever a single building is named: Watchtower 2, Lumberjack 1. That holds on the charter screen, on the card of a foreign settlement, in the messages over buildings, in the names under N and in the chat lines about placing, tearing down, raids and attacks.

  • The number is given when the site is placed, counting from 1 for each kind and settlement, and it stays with the building through every upgrade.
  • A number is never given twice. Tear down Lumberjack 2 and the next lumberjack is Lumberjack 3; the 2 stays a gap.
  • A conquered watchtower takes the next watchtower number of the settlement that conquered it.
  • The charter has no number, because a settlement has only one.

Rules of thumb

A day of the settlement is a Minecraft day. At the full build — two huts, a forester, a sawmill — roughly ten trees felled, ten planted, sixty planks sawn. That takes six residents: four on the posts, one builder and one carrier. An axe lasts about eight settlement days, so replacing gear is a slow trickle rather than a cost you feel.

One carrier keeps up with two huts, a sawmill and a forester with room to spare. A toolmaker on top of that is more than one can carry, and then piles sit around waiting instead of goods flowing. Building a second house — and so getting to eight residents and a second carrier — is the answer.
