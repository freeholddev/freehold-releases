Upgrades

An upgrade raises a standing building to its next level, and the settlement decides when. You do not order one. What you set is the order in which the buildings that are ready get theirs — the upgrade list on the build tab — and how much of your builders an upgrade may take — the lever Upgrade before new building on the politics tab. Every building starts on level 1; twenty-two of them have a level 2.

What level 2 brings depends on the kind of building:

  • A workshop gets a second post — sawmill, toolmaker, charcoal pile, stonemason, windmill, bakery, smelter and smithy. A second resident works there, and the building needs a second tool for them.
  • A building that works the land works at 150 % pace — lumberjack, forester, quarry, opencast quarry, farm, orchard, fishing hut, mine and adit mine. Every job takes two thirds of the time, planting and sowing included.
  • The house gets six beds instead of four, and the storehouse holds 1 800 slots instead of 1 200.
  • The charter holds 3 600 slots instead of 2 400, the watchtower gets a second guard and a second bed, and the builders' yard a third builder's post. The land of the charter and of a watchtower stays the same.

The stock a building keeps grows with it, so a second worker has something to work on. Each level has its own targets on the buildings tab: what you set for level 1 does not hold a level 2 building down.

When a building rises

Three things have to hold at once.

  • It has run a whole day without a problem. For a building with posts, a problem is an empty post, a worker without their tool, or any message that it has stopped or is waiting — has stopped: the settlement has enough X included. So the buildings that rise are the ones that are needed all the time. For a house, every bed has to be taken and food and fuel have to be in it. For a storehouse, the whole store of the settlement has to be at least 75 % full. The charter is never held back. A single problem sets the count back to nothing.
  • Enough goes on around it. The other buildings of your settlement within its radius have to hold enough people between them: every taken post and every resident counts, the building itself does not. The radius and the number are in the table above: a house in the middle of a town needs 12 within 32 blocks, a lumberjack out at its wood 8 within 48. In a settlement of under about twenty residents a house will hardly ever rise.
  • The store holds everything the upgrade costs. The goods have to be there before it goes in the queue, not merely on their way.

The upgrade list

The buildings that are ready for an upgrade stand in a list of their own on the build tab, and their order is yours. It stands under the build queue as Ready for an upgrade, one line a building — House 3, upgrade to level 2 — with what the settlement says about it in grey underneath, such as the goods the store still lacks. The buttons ^ and v move a building one place.

  • The list is kept once a day, at the change of day, and a building that becomes ready is put in by how long it has run without a problem.
  • Only the top of the list is checked, and at most one building goes in the queue a day. If the store holds every good of its next level, it goes in the queue; if something is missing, it waits — and no other building overtakes it. Move a top entry that costs too much for now down with the arrow.
  • A building that is no longer ready drops out of the list — a free bed, no food or fuel, an empty post, a standstill, or surroundings grown too thin — and loses the place you gave it.
  • Roads are not in the list. Which road rises first goes by its traffic — see Road grades.

In the queue

  • Its goods are set aside the moment it goes in the queue. No other building gets them out of the store. The building work starts only once every one of them has been carried to the building.
  • Until the work starts, it can drop out. It is checked again at every change of day; if its surroundings have thinned, a bed has come free or its stock has run out, it leaves the queue, and whatever was already delivered goes back into the store in full.
  • Once the work has started, the upgrade is finished, whatever happens around it.
  • It shows on the build tab, no longer in the upgrade list but in the queue, as House, upgrade to level 2, waiting for its goods, then House, upgrade to level 2, stage 1 of 4, and it is moved up and down the queue like any other site.
  • The building goes on working on level 1 while it is rebuilt. Its workers work and its residents live there. What level 2 brings counts from the moment the last stage is done.
  • A level is never taken back. If the neighbourhood thins out later, the building stays as it is. To be rid of a level, tear the building down and build it anew; torn down, an upgraded building gives back half of both levels.

Upgrade before new building

The third lever on the politics tab decides how your builders divide between upgrades and new buildings. It starts at 50. When both wait at once, upgrades take at most that share of the builders working at buildings — roads are divided off first, by Buildings before roads — and what one side cannot use goes to the other. At 0 nothing is upgraded: no building and no road is checked or put in the queue, and upgrades already in it rest, their goods still set aside, until the lever is above 0 again. The lever divides the builders at roads the same way, between upgrading a road and laying a new one. Under the levers stands how many upgrades are in the queue, road upgrades included, and how many builders are on them. See The charter screen.

What a level 2 looks like

A level 2 stands on the ground of its level 1 and is told by stone and iron. The panels of its walls are masoned, and its lights are lanterns instead of torches, inside and at the door, often on chains. A workshop has the second post at a bench of its own. What else changes is on each building's page.

Lanterns go out like torches when the building stops, and stay dark while it is being built or upgraded — see The fire in a building.

Reading it on the charter screen

The buildings tab says where a building stands and why it is or is not rising. Under its stacks it shows Level 1 of 2., then how many people work and live around it against how many are needed and how long it has run without a problem — without a problem for 12:00 min of 20:00 min, a day being 20 minutes —, and a building with a faster pace says Works at 150% pace.
