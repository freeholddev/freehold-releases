Road grades

A road has one of three grades: earth road, wooden road and stone road. You only ever draw an earth road; the settlement lays wood and then stone over it by itself, on a road that carries enough traffic and already meets the higher grade at one of its ends.

When a road rises

A road rises one grade once it carries enough trips and meets the next grade at one of its ends. Both have to hold at the same time.

  • Traffic. Every trip whose way runs over the road counts, once per trip, out and back together, over the last three days.
  • A connection. At one of the road's two ends there is already the higher grade: a building of level 2 — for wood and for stone alike — or another road of at least that grade. A crossing in the middle of a road is not one of its ends. So wood and stone spread outwards from the busy buildings of level 2, road by road.

The settlement looks once a day. Of the roads that qualify, the one with the most trips goes first, and it goes into the build queue once the store holds all of its goods; those are then set aside for it, as for the upgrade of a building.

The road stays in use while it is being upgraded. The builders lay the new surface a stretch at a time.

Upgrade before new building covers roads as well. On the politics tab of the charter screen it divides the road builders between upgrading roads and laying new ones — and at 0 no road rises either. In the build queue a road upgrade stands as “Wooden Road over a road, waiting for its goods”, then with its stage, and it can be moved like any site.

What it costs

An upgrade costs one plank or one ashlar for each column along the route, not for the three columns of its width: a road 40 columns long costs 40 planks. Columns that another road of that grade already carries cost nothing.

What it looks like

A wooden and a stone road have a middle and two edges, and both take their blocks from the biome they lie in. The earth road is a dirt path all the way across.

  • Wooden road: planks in the middle, and along the edges logs with their bark on, in the dark wood of the biome.
  • Stone road: a paved middle between masoned edges — in most biomes polished andesite between stone bricks, in the desert and the badlands cut sandstone between mud bricks, so the road stands out from the sand.

The forecourt

The forecourt of a building wears the surface of the road that ends at it. Many buildings have a strip of road of their own in front of the door. It is a dirt path at an earth road or with no road at all, and it rises with the road, so the road runs up to the door without a seam. Where several roads end at the same door, the highest grade counts.
