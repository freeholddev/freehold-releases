Drawing a road

Pick Earth Road from the build list — the only road it offers; wooden and stone roads are never drawn, the settlement lays them over a busy earth road that meets the higher grade at one end (see Road grades). You are in the placement mode again, but this time you are drawing a line rather than putting down a box.

A white mark on the ground shows where you are pointing. It sits about four blocks straight ahead of you — you aim by walking and turning, not with the crosshair, exactly as when placing a building. Everything below happens at that mark.

  1. Put the mark on a building and click. That is where the route starts, and it starts at the building's road side — the spot in front of its door, not the middle of it. A road of your settlement is an end as well, built or planned: put the mark anywhere on it and it will snap to the middle of that road. Anything else cannot be an end, and the line under the figures keeps saying so until the mark is on one. See An end on a road for how close to a crossing or a door it may lie.
  2. Click wherever you want the route to bend. Between two points it runs dead straight; there is no path-finding. The bend is the whole decision: round the hill, or straight up it. Lead the route out on the door side of the building: a leg that cuts back across the house is refused, and the columns that do it turn red so you can see where.
  3. Sneak and click on a second building — or on a road — to finish, or press escape to give the route up.

While you draw, the roads of your settlement that are not laid yet lie on the ground as pale blue plates. Click one and the end snaps to that road, as it does on a built one — so several roads and their crossings can be planned before the first of them has laid a block. A road of another settlement is never an end.

While you draw, four figures stand above the hotbar and change with every step you take: how long the route is and how much of it is already paved, what it would cost in work, what in material, and how long a trip takes now against how long it would take then. That last pair is the point of the whole thing.

How the road takes the ground

The drawing on the ground shows the road as it will be built, and the figures above the hotbar include the earth it moves. It makes no difference which of the two ends you draw from: the same route gives the same road and the same price.

  • An earth road is stepped, not flat: each column may rise or fall one block from the one before. A line straight up a hillside cannot get the height onto its length and is refused; the refusal says how many columns it is short. Take a wider bend and it fits.
  • A short rise is cut through instead of climbed, and a low ridge as well. Only a slope that keeps going up is climbed all the way.
  • A shallow hollow is filled instead of dipped into. A deeper or wider one is followed down.
  • A shallow ford is crossed, anything deeper is not. Over a brook the road becomes a causeway, up to three blocks deep. Deeper water is refused, and the refusal says how deep it was. There is no bridge, so lead the route around a pond. The same holds for lava.
  • A road can climb a cliff by cutting into it, and it may run up on piled earth to a building that stands above its surroundings.

The four figures read Length, Work, Material and Travel time. The travel time is given as now → then in seconds or minutes — Travel time: 34 s → 20 s — and says “estimated” where part of the way has not been measured yet; until the route has its second end it has nothing to compare.

Where two roads meet, a crossing appears by itself, once both roads are finished. From the crossing on, both roads are one network, and a trip may run over as many of them as it likes. That is why a road you end on another road is worth building: joining a third building to two others takes one short road to the road that is there, not two more roads from house to house.

An earth road is faster than open ground, a little over twice as fast on the flat, and it makes the whole way level, with no climbing and nothing to walk around. Once it rises to a wooden or stone road it is faster still. What a road saves a trip, and when a carrier takes it, is on Walking on it.

An end on a road

An end on a road keeps 5 blocks from every building and every crossing of the settlement, so that no two crossings and no crossing and door crowd each other. Closer, the end is refused with “The end lies too close to a building or junction …”. Two cases are spared:

  • An end right on a crossing is allowed. The new road joins that crossing — a crossing of three arms becomes one of four.
  • Click near a crossing of the road you are pointing at, and the end lands on it instead of being refused; the drawn line jumps to it so you can see.

Just in front of a building's door, click the building itself instead of the road.
