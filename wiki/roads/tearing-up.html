Tearing up a road

A road is torn up one stretch at a time. Sneak and right-click any block of it — the same gesture as at a building's anchor. What goes is the stretch that block lies in, from crossing to crossing, and what is left of the road on either side stays standing as a road of its own, ending at the crossing it was cut at. A road no crossing touches is one stretch and goes whole.

Sneak and look at a road, and the stretch that would go lies red on the ground. You see it before you ask, so what you are agreeing to is what you can see. Columns that stay red-free are the ones another road carries — you never lose those.

You are asked before anything happens, and nothing does until you say yes. A wooden or stone road gives back half of its material; an earth road gives back nothing, because it costs no material to begin with. A road that is being upgraded also gives back what was delivered to it. What stays is the earth work — the cuttings, the fill, the stepping. Only the surface goes, back to the ground around it. The way between the two ends is then open ground again, a little better than it was before you built, because the ground has been flattened.

Tearing up one road leaves the other alone. Where two of them share columns — at a crossing, or where they leave the same door together — those columns stay, with the surface of the road that stays. And a finished road you ended on the one you tear up is still there afterwards, leading nowhere until you tear it up too.

A road that is not complete goes with the road it ends on. Tear up a road, and every road ending on it that is still waiting or only partly laid goes with it. The red plates show those as well, and the confirmation names them: “The 2 unfinished roads that end on it go with it.”

A road that is still being laid is given up the same way, and it leaves the build list with it.

And a road goes when a building at either of its ends does. Tear down a house that a road runs to, and that road is torn up in the same breath — the whole of it, stretches and all, and the confirmation says how many will go. The unfinished roads that end on one of them go as well, and the confirmation names them in a line of their own.
