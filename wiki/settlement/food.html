Food

This is the one rule in the game that can make your settlement smaller. It is short, and most of it is visible only if you know to look:

  • Houses are delivered to, like a building site. A house wants eight food in it, and a carrier keeps it stocked out of your store, ahead of every workshop. Its fuel is not: that is shared out with the workshops, see Who gets a scarce good. Bread, fish and fruit all count — they are one kind as far as a house is concerned, and each unit feeds the same.
  • Every resident of a house eats one a day. The charter is the exception: its four beds need no food and its people never leave, which is what keeps a settlement without food alive.
  • Nobody moves in without food. A newcomer needs a free bed in a house that has food in it, or a free bed in the charter. A house standing empty with an empty larder says so on the settlement line: "has beds free but no food in any of them". From level 2 on a house needs fuel in its hearth as well — see The fire in a building.
  • A house whose larder stands empty for two days loses one resident. They move into another house that has food if there is a free bed — before any newcomer — and otherwise they leave the settlement, and their post falls vacant. Then the clock starts again, so a house of four empties over eight days rather than all at once.
  • Somebody who loses their roof has the same two days to find a bed that is fed, and leaves if they do not.
  • The settlement warns before the food runs out. When all the food it holds has fallen over three days and at that pace lasts less than three more, a line goes into the chat — “Oakhill: Food is running short. …” — and the food line on the overview turns gold. See Running short.
  • Whoever leaves the settlement is named in the chat, with the reason: “Oakhill: Almut has left the settlement. There was nothing to eat in House 2.” The empty larder itself is said before that, as the blockade “has no food left; its people will move away if none arrives” — see Messages.
  • Nobody dies and nobody works slower. Whoever is still here works at full speed, fed or not.

What that means in practice: one farm and one mill feed one bakery, and one bakery feeds about ten people. A fishing hut or an orchard is the way to food without any of that, and each costs only planks — which is why they are the buildings to put up first if you want to grow before you have a quarry.
