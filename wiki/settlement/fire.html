The fire in a building

Every building's fire tells you whether it is working, and it is the one thing in the settlement you can read from a distance, at night, without clicking anything. Torches, lanterns and hearths go out when the building stops and come back when it starts again. A building of level 1 is lit by torches, one of level 2 by lanterns, inside and out; the lantern is the sign of the higher level you see from outside.

  • A workshop burns while somebody is working in it. It goes dark when its worker goes home: an input missing, its own output pile full, or the store already holding enough of what it makes. A worker waiting for a delivery that is already on its way stays, and so does the light. A building that is merely slow — short of a tool, say — keeps burning, because it has not stopped.
  • A building with no work at all always burns. Your charter, your storehouses and your watchtower have no job anybody could read, so their lights stay on for as long as they stand.
  • A house is lit while somebody lives in it, and its hearth burns while there is fuel as well. The hearth is the one place where the fire costs you something: a house burns one unit of fuel a day, and a carrier keeps two in it out of your store, next to its food. Only an empty house is dark. When the settlement's fuel falls towards nothing, you are warned before it is gone, as with food — see Running short.
  • From level 2 on, a house with a cold hearth takes nobody in. Its free beds stay empty — for newcomers and for anybody moving over from another house — until fuel lies in it again. Whoever already lives there stays, works on at full speed and does not leave. If the only free beds left are cold ones, the settlement says so: “Nobody moves in to X: the free beds are in houses with no fuel.” The answer is fuel — a charcoal pile, or a larger share for the house on the politics tab, see Who gets a scarce good.
  • On level 1 a cold hearth only keeps the house from rising. Nobody leaves, nobody stops working, nobody is turned away, so a young settlement grows before it has any fuel. No monster spawns inside your boundary on its own, lit or not. The charter's overview counts them — how many of your occupied houses have a cold hearth — right under the free beds, so you need not walk the streets to find out.
  • A building that is still going up stays dark, and so does one being upgraded. A lit torch or lantern therefore always means a finished building.
  • A torch or lantern that goes out stays where it is, charred or sooted, without light, and comes back alight when the building starts again.
  • You cannot switch them yourself. They are the settlement's blocks like every other block of a building.
