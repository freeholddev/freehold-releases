Tools

Every filled work place and every builder's place needs one tool, and the building holds them, not the person. A tool counts the jobs it has done, and the cart, which is not a tool of any post, counts trips.

A vanilla tool stands for itself. Axe, hoe, pickaxe and shovel are the wooden vanilla ones, and a vanilla fishing rod counts as a fishing rod — your toolmaker makes it out of planks alone, without string. The shovel is the baker's peel, the collier's spade and the miller's scoop. Saw, hammer, chisel, bellows and brew kettle are Freehold items with a crafting recipe; the brew kettle is also forged by your smithy. The cart has a recipe too. Only axe, fishing rod, saw, hammer and hoe go in at a goods port; the others only if the server allows every good — see Handing goods in.

Your settlement starts equipped — for the chain that makes tools, and for its first food. The charter brings seven tools: the lumberjack's axe, the forester's hoe, the sawmill's saw, a hammer each for the toolmaker and your first builder, and the fishing hut's rod and a second hoe for the orchard. Everything you build after that is equipped by your toolmaker — the two hammers of a builders' yard, the quarry's pickaxe, the stonemason's chisel, the bakery's shovel.

How tools work

  • Missing tools slow work down; they never stop it. A trade working without its tool takes three times as long, a site without a hammer builds three times as slowly, and a building that is half equipped lands in between. The message says "is working with incomplete gear" and names what is missing — that is a hint and not a stoppage, so it is worth reading rather than fixing at once.
  • A building waits if its tool is already on the way. A job keeps the pace it started with, so a lumberjack that sets off one minute before its axe arrives would spend the whole tree at triple time. It therefore stands still until the carrier gets there, says "has not started yet" and names the tool, and then works at full speed. If the settlement has no axe at all, the felling starts anyway and is simply slow.
  • Tools wear out and break. A tool counts finished pieces of work, not time: an axe survives forty fellings, which is about eight settlement days for a lumberjack at work all day. A hammer counts finished building stages, which is several finished buildings. The toolmaker makes new ones out of planks, so a settlement that has one keeps itself equipped without you. A worn tool you hand in counts as a whole one.
  • You can make the five basic ones yourself and hand them into the goods port, exactly like planks: axe, fishing rod, saw, hammer and hoe. The charter's kit already covers a slow start; handing tools in is the way to run ahead of your toolmaker — and if you ever do run the settlement dry, the hammer is the one to make first: it is the tool of the toolmaker and of every builder's place there is.

Recipes

None of these recipes shows in the recipe book, not even once you hold the ingredients — this page is where they stand; the only Freehold recipe in the book is the charter. All of them are made at a crafting table, and all are shaped. P is any planks, S a stick, I an iron ingot:

    Saw            Hammer         Chisel        Bellows       Brew kettle      Cart

  P  P  P        P  P  P            P            P  P  S        S  I  S        P     P
     S           P  S  P            S            P  P  S        I     I        P  P  P
                    S                                           I  I  I        S  I  S

The cart is not a tool: it belongs to a carrier, not to a post. A carrier without one takes it out of the store. A cart you make yourself goes in only if the server allows every good; otherwise carts come from your toolmaker once your smithy makes fittings.

A soldier's spear, shield, sword and armour are gear, not tools: they wear out by the day — see Soldiers and gear.
