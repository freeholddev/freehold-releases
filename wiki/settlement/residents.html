Residents

How people move in

Your charter brings four people; after that newcomers come on their own, and faster the more supplied beds the settlement has. One newcomer a day comes from the charter alone. Every bed in a supplied house — food in its store, and from level 2 fuel in its hearth — adds to the pace, whether it is taken or free, and a bed in a house of a higher level counts for more. Every bed of a connected village adds as well, the more the better your reputation there. As a guide, sixty beds on level 2 bring a little over two newcomers a day. The exact numbers are on the charter's page.

While a settlement fills its first house, newcomers come four times as fast. That holds as long as the settlement has fewer residents than its charter and its first house have beds — eight at the start — so the first house is full in about a day. A server can change the factor, see Game rules.

A newcomer from a connected village can be seen walking in: from the village's bell, over the link road and through the gate. If the bed they were coming for is gone by then, they turn back and come again later.

  • Nobody moves in without a fed bed. A newcomer needs a free bed in a house with food in it, or a free bed in the charter. The overview of the charter screen says how many beds are free.
  • From level 2 on, the house needs fuel as well. A house of level 2 with a cold hearth takes nobody in, see The fire in a building.
  • Newcomers are not saved up. While no such bed is free nobody is on the way, so a house built late does not bring a crowd at once.
  • Somebody who has lost their bed comes first. A resident who had to leave a house takes a free fed bed before any newcomer does.

Who does what

Every resident is a carrier, a builder, a worker or a soldier. The overview of the charter screen counts each, and those without a post. Whoever holds a post in a watchtower or a barracks is a soldier — see Soldiers and gear.

  • Carriers come first. The settlement keeps one carrier for every four residents, and never fewer than one, before anybody is given a building site or a bench — see Goods and carriers.
  • A settlement never loses its last carrier. If every carrier is gone — moved out of a house without food, say — a builder becomes a carrier at once, or a worker if there is no builder. A soldier never does. So a settlement whose food ran out recovers by itself once there is food in the store again: the carrier brings it into the houses, and people move in again.
  • Builders come next, as many as there are builder's posts and as the politics tab lets through — see The charter screen.
  • Workers take the free posts that have work, within reach of where they live — see Where people live. When several posts are free, the settlement picks by trade, in an order you set on the politics tab — see Where a free resident goes. It starts as the table below, with food first and the tavern last, so a settlement short of hands has a tavern without a keeper, and that tavern stays shut. A post with nothing to do is not taken.
  • The order is only for residents without a post. A worker who already has one is not moved to another.

Where people live

A worker lives within reach of their post: at most 64 blocks between the house and the building they work at, in a straight line and ignoring height. Every building with beds counts as a house, the charter included. Carriers and builders need no reach: they work all over the settlement and live wherever a bed is free. A soldier sleeps where they serve. You assign nobody; the settlement sorts it out itself.

  • Whoever already lives somewhere works near home first. A resident without a post takes one within reach of their house. Only if none is free there do they take one elsewhere, and move. So no quarter runs empty because there is much to do in another.
  • A newcomer picks the work first, then the bed near it — the nearest free bed to that post, in a house with food. If the beds in reach are all taken, a carrier or a builder living there makes room, provided another bed is free for them.
  • A worker who lives too far away and finds no bed within reach gives up the post after a day. That happens when their house is torn down, for instance. Until then the building says that a worker lives too far away.
  • Your answer to an empty post is a house nearby. A post nobody can take from a bed in reach stays empty, and the building says why: either no house stands within reach of it — build one — or, while somebody is out of work, no house within reach has a supplied bed free. Then the houses there are full, and another house or an upgrade helps, or one has beds but no food, and the answer is food. A post that stays empty without a message is waiting for people: the settlement is short of hands, and a house does not help. The messages are hints, shown over the building and on the buildings tab — see Messages.

Work, jobs and beds

  • The people are decoration; the work is not. Everything below about residents is about what you see, never about what the settlement produces. Walk away and the same planks are sawn at the same moment with nobody there to saw them.
  • Nobody changes jobs. A resident keeps their post for good, unless they live out of its reach — see Where people live.
  • A post with a bed in the same building keeps it for its own worker. The guard of a watchtower sleeps in the tower, and one of the charter's four beds belongs to its builder. Losing the post does not cost anyone their bed.

The people walking around

Residents appear once you are within about 128 blocks of one of the settlement's buildings, and they are gone again when you leave. They are figures showing you what the settlement is doing — which is why they behave in ways no ordinary mob does:

  • They cannot be hurt or killed. Not by you, not by a mob, not by lava.
  • A detour costs nothing. Someone who had to go the long way round starts work late, and the job still ends on time. Wall one in and they step across the obstacle by vanishing over it.
  • Work that changes the world happens where it changes it. The lumberjack stands at the tree as it comes down and the forester over the spot where the sapling appears. Work that changes nothing stays indoors: the sawyer saws at the bench.
  • Some workshops have two places to work at, and the worker moves from one to the other halfway through a job — the smith from the hearth to the anvil, the baker from the trough to the table. On level 2 the bakery, the smithy and the toolmaker have two workers, one at each place.
  • Every worker takes two breaks a day and eats — outside the door, or where they stand if they work outdoors. A break costs nothing: no food is used up, and the job takes no longer.
  • They hold the tool of their trade while they work, and swing it. Empty hands mean they are on their way somewhere. The tool that wears out is the one the building holds, not the one in their hand.
  • You can hear them work — wood at the tree, stone at the rock, the saw in the sawmill, the hammer in the smithy. The volume follows the Friendly Creatures slider.
  • They open wooden doors and close them behind. Iron doors they cannot open, which makes an iron door the way to shut a room for good.
  • They walk round a finished building, not over it, unless there is no other way.
  • Names and faces are fixed. Each one keeps the same name and the same vanilla skin for good, the same for everyone on the server. A datapack can replace both.
  • The one carrying something is a carrier, and it holds what it is carrying. It sets the load down at the front of the building, where the road meets it, and never goes inside. Between two trips its hands are empty.
  • A carrier pushes its cart only while it is loaded. On the way to the goods and on the way back its hands are free — the cart still counts all the while, see Goods and carriers.
  • A trip is two walks: to the goods, then with them. A carrier that has just delivered somewhere else walks to the goods first, empty-handed, and you can watch it do it.
  • Soldiers and the trader stand at their post when there is nothing to do, day and night. Soldiers walk a round at night, a torch in hand. The trader walks to the village and back on every trip, and a soldier steps out to meet raiders — see Trade Post and Raids.
  • Nobody sleeps, and where they wait says why. With no job to do they stand at their work place when the hold-up is already being cleared — a tool on its way over — and at their bed when it needs you, like a feller with no trees left in reach. A carrier waits where its last delivery left it; at a building that is not a store at most two wait, and the others walk back to the nearest store.
