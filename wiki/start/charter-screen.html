The charter screen

The charter screen is where the settlement is read. V opens it wherever you stand, and the key can be moved in the controls menu under "Freehold". Which settlement it shows follows from where you are: the one whose territory you are standing in, and outside anybody's territory the one you looked at last. Tabs down the left edge switch between what it holds.

What you see of a settlement depends on the role you hold in it. The freeholder and the members see the whole screen and work its buttons. A visitor reads every tab but changes nothing: the buttons are grey, and so is the Build tab. Whoever holds no role in a settlement sees only its card.

It stays live while it is open. The numbers keep moving, the game does not stop, and a carrier that arrives while you are reading changes the figure under your eyes.

Overview

Where the key lands: the state of the settlement at a glance, and everything it is waiting for, gathered in one list instead of floating over the roofs one at a time. From top to bottom:

  • People: how old the settlement is, how many residents it has and what they do — builders, carriers, workers, soldiers, without a post —, how many beds are free, how many houses have a cold hearth, and how many carriers it has against how many it needs.
  • Food and fuel: how much is in stock and how that has changed over three days — Food: 41 in stock, down 12 over 3 days. —, in gold while it is running short.
  • Land and villages: how many chunks the settlement holds, and every village near it with its beds, its distance and whether it is connected.
  • Raids and attacks: the threat against its threshold or the grace still left, every raid and attack under way with its target and arrival, and how the last one ended.
  • What it is waiting for, which says Nothing to report when all is well.

The list is ordered by kind: first Standing until you act, the blockades; then Already being cleared, what the settlement is sorting out itself; last Could go better, the hints. The button under the heading sets which messages also go into the chat — Chat: blockades only, the default, Chat: blockades and waiting, or Chat: off. See Messages. Two more buttons are about raids: Raids in chat: on/off and Raid markers: on/off, both on by default — see Raids. All three hold for your game only.

Build

What is already in the queue and how far each site has come, then what else can be built, what it costs, and what you have. Hold the cursor over an entry to read what the building is for.

A building is grey and cannot be picked while a good it costs cannot reach the settlement — none of it lies in the store, you may not hand it in, no building of yours makes it and no trade post buys it. A building that makes it counts from the moment it is placed, so a chain can be planned ahead. The tooltip of a grey entry names what is missing: “Nothing in this settlement makes Ashlar yet, and it cannot be handed in. Place a building that makes it first.” In a new settlement the stonemason opens most of the grey buildings — see Production chains. On a server that allows every good to be handed in, nothing is grey.

The queue order is yours: the two small buttons beside a site move it one place up or down, and builders always go to the first site of its kind on the list that can be worked on. A site only holds so many — two at a small building, three at a middling one, four at a large — so once you have more builders than the first site can use, the rest walk on to the next one and your settlement raises two at a time. How they divide between buildings and roads is the politics tab's, and a line under the queue says how it currently stands. Upgrades stand in the queue as well, as House 3, upgrade to level 2, waiting for its goods and then with their stage, and move up and down like any other site — see Upgrades. Under the queue stands the list Ready for an upgrade: the buildings that may rise and are not in the queue yet, each with what the settlement says about it in grey. Its order is yours too, with the same two buttons, and the top one is the next to go on the queue — see The upgrade list. There is no button to cancel a site — to give one up, sneak and right-click it in the world, the same as tearing down anything else. Until its anchor block is built, any block of the site will do, the levelled ground included; after that, only the anchor.

Store

Every good in the store: how many there are, how many slots the pile takes, and how many more a carrier is holding on the way in. There is no other place to read the store, and nothing can be taken out of it by hand. The cursor over a row says what the good is.

The goods stand under headings — wood, stone, food, iron, tools, supplies, defence and trade — and only goods that are in the store or on the way in are shown.

Buildings

Every building on the left under its name and number, such as Watchtower 2, and the one you pick on the right: who works there, what is on the bench, and both of its own stacks against the numbers they are measured by. A stack under its target is a carrier that is owed. Those two numbers are yours to set, with the small buttons beside each line — hold shift for ten at a time, and the star resets one to its default. A target you set holds for every building of that kind on the same level as the one shown, the ones already standing and the ones you have not built yet: turn a sawmill of level 1 up and every sawmill of level 1 wants more. A building that rises to level 2 goes by what is set for level 2, so set both levels if you want both changed, each at a building of that level. The star puts a target back to the default of its level and is grey while the target stands there. A threshold holds for every building of that kind, whatever its level. The buttons grey out where the number cannot go: a good the building actually consumes never drops to nothing, and a threshold never rises so far that a finished job would have nowhere to put its result.

A button above the list chooses which kind of building it shows. It reads All, or the chosen kind with its count, Trade post (3); a click opens the list of kinds to choose from. The filter is not kept: every time the screen opens it reads All.

The list and the building on the right scroll each on their own, with the scroll wheel over the one you want to move.

A building site shows its bill instead, one line per good under Comes in:, such as Torch: 11 of 16. The first number is what the site has of that good — built in already plus lying at the site —, the second the whole bill; what is missing is the difference. What a carrier is bringing right now is on the line On the way here: …. These lines have no buttons, since a bill is not something you set, and nothing stands under Goes out:. That matters most for goods that never pass the store: a carrier takes torches from the toolmaker straight to a watchtower's site, so a store without torches does not mean none are being made.

It says what the building is doing — At work, 1:05 min to go. — and, depending on the building, more: a trade post its partner village, when the trader is back and its purchase targets under Buys up to:; a village gate its village and the soldiers who stand by it; a watchtower or barracks every soldier with their health and the days their gear has left, and (away on an attack) for one who is out on an attack. Every time on the charter screen is real time: seconds under a minute (45 s), minutes and seconds under an hour (2:05 min), hours and minutes above (1:30 h), as long as the server runs at full speed.

It also says where the building stands on its way up: its level (Level 1 of 2.), how many people work and live around it against how many are needed, how long it has run without a problem, its pace if it is faster, and one line on why it is or is not being upgraded. Every one of those lines is on Upgrades.

A building from an older version of its drawing has a button Lift to current drawing next to Done. It rebuilds the building to today's drawing, for nothing, so that the settlement repairs it again — see What the settlement repairs.

Politics

The politics tab holds three levers, two rankings and the ore share of the mines: the standing rules you set rather than manage.

The levers

Each lever is a percentage with the same three small buttons. Build before production is how much of your workforce takes a building site instead of a work bench: at 100 everybody who can build, builds; at 0 a single builder always stays. Buildings before roads is how your builders divide between the two kinds of site in the queue: at 100 a road is only built while no building site can be worked on, and what the division leaves over always goes to the buildings. Upgrade before new building is how much of the builders an upgrade may take while upgrades and new sites wait together, at buildings and at roads alike; it starts at 50, and the star resets it to that rather than to 100. At 0 nothing is upgraded at all, no building and no road — see Upgrades and Road grades. Under the levers stands what they are doing right now — who is posted where, who is standing on which kind of site, what is in the queue to divide them over, and how many upgrades are in it with how many builders. A policy is a rule and not an order: with one builder, or with no road on the queue, a dial can move and nothing will follow, and those lines are how you see why.

Where a free resident goes

A list of every kind of building with work posts, the first at the top: a resident without a post takes the first free post on it that has work. The arrows ↑ and ↓ move a line one place; the star by the heading resets the starting order. Each line says how many posts of its kind are held, “2. Fishing Hut · 2 of 3 posts held”; a kind your settlement has not built yet stands grey with not built. When a settlement loses its last carrier, the worker on the lowest post of this list becomes the carrier. What the order starts as and how posts are filled is on Residents.

Who gets a scarce good

For every good two or more kinds of building use, each of these kinds has a share in per cent, “Sawmill: 70 %”, with - and + beside it: five at a time, twenty-five with Shift. Your building sites are one of these kinds, “Building sites: 75 %”, wherever a workshop works with a good they are built with. A good's shares always add up to 100: giving one kind more takes it from the others in proportion to their shares — raise the smelter's fuel from 27 to 32, and the smithy and the toolmaker have 17 each, the bakery 13, the brewery 12 and the house 9. The star stands by the good, Fuel or Raw Wood, and puts all its kinds back to their starting shares. How a share works out, and what the shares start at, is on Who gets a scarce good.

What a mine digs

Every mine digs coal and iron ore in the proportion you set here, shown as “Digging coal: 50 %” and “Digging iron ore: 50 %”; the two always add up to 100, and they start at 50 each. It is one setting for the whole settlement, for the mine and the adit mine alike. Turning one ore up turns the other down: a click on - or + moves five, Shift twenty-five, and the star by the heading resets them to 50 to 50.

An ore the settlement has enough of, or that a mine has none of left, is skipped, and the other one takes the work. At 0 an ore is never dug; a mine that could dig it says so — see Mine. The section only shows once a mine stands.

Attack

Every foreign watchtower your soldiers reach, the nearest first — and a foreign charter, once no tower of its settlement stands — each with an attack line: which target, out of which of your buildings' reach, how many soldiers can go and how far it is. − and + set how many go, and Attack with N sends them. A line that cannot be used is grey and says why. Only the freeholder and the members see the lines. Everything about it is on Attacks.

Members

Every role in the settlement, the freeholder first, then the members and the visitors, each by name. Everybody with a role sees the tab, a visitor too. Roles are given out and taken away by the freeholder alone; everybody else sees those buttons grey.

  • The freeholder offers a role. Under the list stand the players who are online and hold no role in this settlement, each with the buttons Member and Visitor. A click sends an offer and does nothing else: the freeholder reads “You offered X the role Y in Z. It counts once they accept.”
  • The role counts once the player accepts it. They get a line in chat with [Accept] and [Decline] to click; accepting gives them the role at once and their charter screen in place of the card, and a refusal is reported to the freeholder. While an offer is open, the player's line reads invited as … instead of the buttons, and nobody can offer them a second one. An offer lapses after five minutes. A role also binds: nobody may attack a settlement they hold a role in — see Attacks.
  • Leaving and taking away work at once. A member or visitor leaves with Leave on their own line; the freeholder takes any role but their own with Remove. The player gets a line in chat and from then on sees only the settlement's card.
  • To change a role — a member into a visitor, say — take it away and offer the new one: a role is only offered to a player who holds none in the settlement.

The card

A settlement you hold no role in shows you a card instead of its charter screen, headed A settlement you hold no role in. It says the settlement's name and Owner: …. Right-click the anchor of one of its buildings with an empty hand and the card names that building as well, Building: Watchtower 2; V on its land opens the same card without a building. Its store, its policies, its build list and its buildings stay hidden.

Under a watchtower or the charter stand the lines to attack it, one for every settlement of yours whose soldiers reach it, with the same −, + and Attack with N as on the Attack tab. The line under a charter stays grey while a watchtower of its settlement stands — see Attacks.
