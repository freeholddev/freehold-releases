Glossary

The words this wiki uses, A to Z, each with the page that explains it. Buildings and goods have their own lists: All buildings and All goods.

Access
The invisible link from a building to a road that passes near it, at the point of the road nearest its door. See Walking on it.
Anchor block
The block of carved stone inside every building, one each. Right-click it to open the building on the charter screen, sneak and right-click to tear the building down. See Controls.
Ashlar
Squared stone from the stonemason; its item is stone bricks. The masonry buildings are built with it.
Attack
One settlement sending its soldiers against a watchtower of another, or against its charter once no tower of it stands that has had a guard. See Attacks.
Band
The layers of rock a mine works: its ore has to lie there to count. See Mine and Adit Mine.
Basic good
A good you may hand in at a goods port: planks, logs, food, and the axe, fishing rod, saw, hammer and hoe. The settlement makes everything else itself. See Handing goods in.
Blockade
A message that stands until you act. By default it is the only kind that goes into the chat. See Messages.
Boundary
The edge of your land, marked by march stones. Inside it, nothing changes a block but you and your members, and nobody else uses what stands there. See Boundary and protection.
Build queue
The list of sites, upgrades and roads waiting to be built, on the Build tab of the charter screen. You set its order. See The charter screen.
Builder
A resident on a builder's post, raising the sites on the build queue. The charter has one post, every builders' yard two more.
Building number
The number a building carries among the buildings of its kind in its settlement, as in Watchtower 2. Given once and never again. See Building numbers.
Card
What you see of a settlement you hold no role in, instead of its charter screen: its name, its owner, the building you clicked, and the lines to attack it. See The card.
Carrier
A resident who moves goods between buildings and the store. A settlement keeps one for every four residents, and never fewer than one. See Goods and carriers.
Cart
A carrier's hand cart: 36 slots of weight a trip instead of 12. See Tools.
Ceiling
How much of a good the settlement wants ready; above it, nobody makes more. It grows with the number of buildings that use the good. See Only so much is made.
Charter
The item that founds a settlement, and the building it becomes: store, first beds and first land. See Charter.
Charter screen
The screen where the settlement is read and steered, opened with V. See The charter screen.
Claim
The land a charter or a watchtower holds, counted in whole chunks around the chunk it stands in: 7×7 for the charter, 9×9 for a watchtower.
Connected village
A village joined to your settlement by a village gate and a road to it. Only a connected village sends people, trades and is protected. See Villages.
Conquest
Winning an attack on a watchtower: the tower and the land only it held go to the attacker, and what the defender had on that land is torn down. See A conquered watchtower.
Crossing
Where two roads meet. It appears by itself, joins the roads into one network and is marked by a road lantern that shows the grade of its highest road — except right at a building's door. See Once the route is finished.
Duel
One raider or attacker against one soldier, blow for blow, until one falls. A raid and an attack are fought as duels, on several duel places at once. See Raids.
Emerald
The coin of the trade with villages. A trade post earns it by selling and spends it by buying; it also crafts the charter.
Fed bed
A free bed in a house that has food in it, or a free bed in the charter. Newcomers only move into one. See Food.
Food
Bread, fish and fruit — one kind as far as a house is concerned. Beer is not food.
Forecourt
The strip of road in front of a building's door. It wears the surface of the road that ends at it. See The forecourt.
Freehold
A settlement and its land; also the name of the mod.
Freeholder
The player who founded a settlement. They may do everything in it, and they alone give out roles — see Members.
Fuel
Coal or charcoal. Burnt in house hearths and at the fires of the toolmaker, bakery, brewery, smelter and smithy. Made by the charcoal pile and by a mine on coal.
Gear
What a soldier wears: a weapon (spear or sword), a shield and armour. It wears out by the day. See Soldiers and gear.
Goods
Everything the settlement stores, makes and uses, tools included. See All goods.
Goods port
The barrel-like block in the outside wall of the charter and of every storehouse. Right-click it with a basic good in hand to put it into the store; a hopper works too. Nothing comes back out.
Grace period
The days after its founding in which no raid comes for a settlement and no other settlement may attack it. See Raids and Attacks.
Grade
What a road is made of: earth, wood or stone. You draw earth; the settlement raises a busy road by itself once the higher grade meets it at one end. See Road grades.
Hint
A message over a building that is working, only worse than it could. It never goes to the chat. See Messages.
Level
How far a building has been upgraded (not a mine's levels, the layers its galleries lie on — see Mine). Every building starts on level 1, and twenty-two of them can rise to level 2. See Upgrades.
Lift
Rebuilding a building from an older version of its drawing to the current one, so that the settlement repairs it again. See What the settlement repairs.
Link road
The earth road a village gate lays from itself into the village — the one road outside your land, and no part of your road network. See Village Gate.
Manned watchtower
A watchtower whose post a guard has taken at least once. Only a manned tower claims land, can be attacked and raided, and shields the charter; it stays manned when its post falls empty later. See Watchtower.
March stone
A stone on every chunk corner of your boundary. See March stones.
Mining area
The ground a mine or an adit mine works, seen from above. The areas of two mines never overlap. See Mine.
Ore map
The coloured fields over your claim while you choose a mine, one per chunk, showing how much ore of one seam a shaft there would find. See Mine.
Ore share
How much of its digging a mine gives to each of its two seams, in per cent; the two add up to 100. One setting for the whole settlement. See What a mine digs.
Pace
How fast a building works, in per cent. A building that works the land works at 150 % on level 2, and every job takes two thirds of the time. See Upgrades.
Pile
A building's own stock: one pile for what has been delivered to it, one for what it has made and nobody has fetched yet. See Goods do not teleport.
Placement mode
The raised view while you place a building or draw a road: walk to move, scroll to turn, right-click to place. See Placing a building.
Policy
One of the three levers on the politics tab of the charter screen: Build before production, Buildings before roads and Upgrade before new building. See Politics.
Post
A place for one resident in a building: a work place or a builder's post. Nobody ever changes post. See Residents.
Raid
Raiders coming for a settlement or a connected village. Soldiers meet them in duels; if all fall, the raiders carry goods out of the store. See Raids.
Raid marker
The red label and the red beam over the target of a raid, from its announcement to its end. See Raids.
Raider
One of a raid's band: a figure in the shape of a vindicator with an axe that attacks nothing but your soldiers and cannot be hurt. See Raids.
Ranking
One of the two lists on the politics tab of the charter screen: where a free resident goes, and who gets a scarce good. See Politics.
Raw wood
The good the lumberjack fells, handed in as any logs. The tables count it in logs.
Reach
How far from its building a trade works in the world — the lumberjack's trees, the farm's fields, the tavern's houses. Also called its work radius. A worker, too, lives within reach of their post: at most 64 blocks from it. See Where people live.
Reputation
How well your settlement stands with a connected village. Trade raises it, every day lowers it, and the higher it is, the more people the village sends. See Villages.
Resident
A person of the settlement: a carrier, a builder, a worker, a soldier, or somebody without a post yet.
Road
A way you draw between two buildings, or from a building to a road, that the builders then lay. See Drawing a road.
Role
What a player is in a settlement: its freeholder, who may do everything; a member, who builds, tears down and sets the policies; or a visitor, who reads the charter screen, changes nothing and uses nothing on the land but doors and fence gates. Whoever holds no role sees the settlement's card. The freeholder offers roles, and a player has one once they accept — see Members.
Rubble
The stone the quarries break out; its item is cobblestone. The stonemason squares it into ashlar.
Seam
The iron or the coal in a mine's band. A mine works both in the proportion set on the politics tab, and each runs out on its own.
Share
What one kind of building gets of a good two or more kinds use, in per cent; a good's shares add up to 100. The building sites together count as one kind wherever a workshop uses a good they are built with. While the good is scarce, the next load goes to the kind furthest behind its share. Set on the politics tab. See Who gets a scarce good.
Shield
The time after a conquest in which the conquered watchtower cannot be attacked again. Raiders still come for it. See Attacks.
Shortage
Food or fuel that, at the pace of the last three days, runs out within three more: the settlement says so once in the chat and picks the stock out in gold on the overview. See Running short.
Sim tick
The unit the datapack counts time in: one a second, twenty game ticks, 1 200 to a Minecraft day. The game itself shows every time in seconds, minutes and hours — see The datapack.
Site
A building that is placed but not finished. Its ground is levelled first, then it goes up in stages.
Slot
The unit of room in the store and on a carrier. Every good has a weight in slots.
Soldier
A resident on a post in a watchtower or a barracks. Only soldiers fight in a raid or an attack. See Soldiers and gear.
Stage
One part of a building's work. Each stage takes its share of the material before its blocks go up. See How a building goes up.
Standstill
A building that cannot go on. The message floats over the roof, and a blockade — one that needs you — goes to the chat unless you have turned that off. See Messages.
Store
The settlement's one stock of goods, measured in slots, reached through every goods port. You can put goods in, not take them out.
Stretch
A piece of road between two crossings, or between a crossing and an end. A road is torn up one stretch at a time.
Surroundings
The taken posts and residents of the other buildings within a radius of a building. A building rises to its next level only once its surroundings hold enough people. See When a building rises.
Target and threshold
The two numbers each kind of building works by: how much of its inputs it keeps in stock, and how much of its output it holds before a carrier fetches it. A target is set per kind and level, a threshold per kind. Both can be set on the Buildings tab.
Territory
All the land of a settlement: the claims of its charter and its watchtowers together.
Theme
A second set of drawings and materials a settlement builds with instead of Freehold's own, chosen at the founding. It changes how the buildings look, never what they do. See Themes.
Threat
What grows every day in a settlement and in every connected village until a raid comes for it. See Raids.
Tool
The piece a post works with. Without it the work takes three times as long. See Tools.
Upgrade
A standing building rebuilt to its next level, which the settlement starts on its own. See Upgrades.
Upgrade list
The buildings ready for an upgrade, under the build queue on the Build tab. You set its order; the top one is the next to go in the queue. See The upgrade list.
Version
One of the looks a building comes in. You choose it while placing, with sneak and the scroll wheel; it never changes how the building works.
Village
A village the world generated. Near your boundary it can be connected with a village gate; inside it, it is refused. See Villages.
Work unit
How building work is counted. A builder with a hammer adds one a second, without one a third of that.
Worker
A resident on a building's work place.
