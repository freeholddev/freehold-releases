Founding a settlement

  1. Hold the charter — crafted from an emerald and eight planks, see Items and blocks. The view swings up and behind you and looks down — that is the placement mode, and it lasts as long as you are placing something. A model of the building stands a few paces in front of you, with the outline of the claim it would take — 7×7 chunks, 112 blocks across.
  2. Walk to move it, look around to swing it around you. The building keeps its distance ahead of you; there is no crosshair to aim with. Moving the mouse up or down tilts the view and does not move the building: flatter to see a tall building whole, steeper to look down on its footprint.
  3. Scroll to turn it. The pale yellowish post where the road will meet the building marks its front, and a pale strip runs to it from the door, so you can see which way the building faces. Green means the spot is allowed; red says why in words, on a line above the hotbar next to the gestures ("the ground varies by more than 3 blocks over the site"). The reason stands as long as the spot is refused, so you can walk until it goes.
  4. Right-click to place, type a name of up to 32 characters, confirm. The name stays; only an operator can change it later — see Commands. If the world offers more than one theme, a button Theme: … under the name field steps through them before you confirm; the theme decides how your buildings look, never what they do. The charter builds itself in about a minute, without material or a builder, and brings 4 residents. For ten seconds a yellow line on the ground shows your new claim, and the march stones stand on it.

Where you may found

A freehold is founded in the Overworld only. In the Nether, the End or any other dimension the charter in your hand shows no outline, and a right-click with it says above the hotbar “A freehold can only be founded in the Overworld.”

Three unclaimed chunks have to stay between your future boundary and every other freehold's land. From another charter that is 10 chunks, from another freehold's watchtower 11, because a tower claims more land around itself. The refusal names the distance in blocks.

A server can add two limits of its own: how many settlements one player may own, and a distance every boundary has to keep from the world spawn. Both are off unless an operator turns them on — see Game rules.

Where you stand inside your chunk decides how your territory sits. The claim is counted in whole chunks around the chunk the charter stands in, not measured from the charter itself — so a charter near a chunk corner ends up as much as 15 blocks off the middle of its own land. The outline in your hand shows this before you place, and stepping a few blocks is all it takes to centre yourself.

A village near your boundary can be connected and then adds to your newcomers, but you may not found on top of one — see Villages. At the founding the chat names every village found near the boundary: “A village of 8 beds lies near the boundary. A village gate beside it and a road to the gate connect it, and then people move in from it.”

Your first planks

The charter brings tools, but no material. A new settlement starts with its four residents and a kit of tools for its first posts — the lumberjack's axe, the forester's hoe, the sawmill's saw, hammers for the toolmaker and the first builder, and the fishing rod and second hoe for the first fishing hut and orchard; the exact kit is on the charter's page. It owns no planks, no food and no fuel. Until you hand planks into the goods port, nothing can be built: the first planks are yours to bring.
