First steps

From the charter to a settlement that feeds itself and grows on its own. Every step is short and leads to the page with the details; the order is one that works, not the only one.

1. Get a charter

The charter is the item that founds a settlement, and you craft it from an emerald and eight planks: the emerald in the middle of the crafting table, planks of any wood all round. It shows in the recipe book as soon as you carry an emerald — trade one from a villager or mine it in the mountains. In creative mode it is the first entry in the Freehold tab. See Items and blocks.

2. Find a spot and found

Hold the charter and the view changes to the placement mode. A model of the charter stands ahead of you with the outline of the land it will claim, 7×7 chunks. Walk to move it, scroll to turn it, right-click to place, then give the settlement a name. Look for:

  • ground that is level within three blocks where the charter stands;
  • a forest inside the claim, because wood is what everything starts with;
  • water for a fishing hut, and a hillside or a rocky plain for a quarry later on;
  • a village within 48 blocks of the boundary but not inside it — once you connect it with a village gate, it sends you people and trades with you. See Villages.

The charter builds itself in about a minute and brings four residents and a kit of tools. See Founding a settlement.

3. Hand in the first planks

The charter brings tools but no material, so the first planks are yours. Hold a stack of planks and right-click the goods port, the barrel-like block in the charter's outside wall. A lumberjack and a sawmill take 56 planks between them. Only basic goods go in: planks, logs, food and the five simplest tools; everything else the settlement makes itself. Nothing comes back out of the store — you can give, not take. See Handing goods in.

4. Build the wood chain

Open the charter screen with V, pick a building on the Build tab and place it like the charter. A building shown grey costs something nothing in your settlement can make yet; its tooltip says what. Start with a lumberjack near the trees and a sawmill near both it and the charter. From then on the settlement makes its own planks.

Your four residents are exactly enough: one carries, one builds, one fells and one saws. Each building needs its own worker, and every settlement keeps one carrier per four residents before anyone else gets a post — see Who does what.

5. Make room for people

Newcomers only move into a free bed with food in its house. The charter's four beds are full from the start, so growth needs a house and something to eat. The quick ways to food cost planks only: a fishing hut on open water, or an orchard, which plants its own trees and needs nothing delivered. The charter's kit already holds the fishing rod and the hoe they work with. While the first house fills, newcomers come quickly — it is full in about a day; after that somebody new arrives about once a day, sooner the more fed beds you keep. See Food and How people move in.

Build houses near the work. A worker lives within 64 blocks of their post — the charter counts as a house too — so a hut far out needs a house near it, or nobody can take its post. See Where people live.

6. Keep the wood coming

  • A forester replants what the lumberjack fells; without one the forest around the hut runs out.
  • A toolmaker replaces worn tools and equips every building you add. A building without its tool works three times as slowly, but it never stops.
  • A second lumberjack — two huts keep one sawmill busy.
  • A builders' yard when the build queue grows: two more builders.

7. Branch out

Each chain opens the next. Stone first: a quarry and a stonemason make ashlar, which the farm, the mill, the bakery, the smelter and the smithy are built with. A charcoal pile makes fuel, fuel makes torches, and torches build a watchtower that pushes your boundary out once a resident takes its guard's post. Iron gives fittings, and fittings the brewery and the tavern. What needs what is on Production chains.

A village nearby is worth a gate. A village gate on the edge of your land and a road to it connect the village: it sends people, and a trade post can sell it your surplus for emeralds and buy bread, fish, fuel or iron with them.

8. Draw roads

A road does not make delivery possible; it makes it faster. Once your buildings stand far apart or across rough ground, draw roads between them — or from a building to a road that is already there. See Drawing a road.

9. Get ready for raids

After ten days raiders start to come, more often the bigger the settlement is. Only soldiers fight them: the guard of a watchtower and the four soldiers of a barracks. Your toolmaker makes them a spear and a shield as soon as it stands, and the smithy later a sword and armour. Without a single soldier the raiders simply help themselves to your store. See Raids.

On a server with other players, the same ten days are your grace against them. After that a settlement of another player whose soldiers reach one of your watchtowers may attack it and take it with its land — see Attacks.

Watching it run

  • A line in the chat means you have to do something. Anything the settlement is sorting out itself only shows as a marker over the roof — unless you choose to have that in the chat too. See Messages.
  • The overview of the charter screen lists everything the settlement is waiting for in one place. See The charter screen.
  • A lit building is working, a dark one is not — readable from across the valley at night. See The fire in a building.
