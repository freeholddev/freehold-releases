Raids

A raid is a band of raiders that comes for a settlement or for a village it has connected, whether anybody is watching or not. Your soldiers meet them one by one; if they all fall, the raiders carry goods out of your store. A raid takes nothing else: it conquers nothing and damages nothing. A server can switch raids off — see Game rules. One settlement sending its soldiers against another is something else — see Attacks.

When a raid comes

Every settlement has a threat that grows every day, and a raid comes when it is full. After a grace period from the founding the threat grows at every change of day by a fixed amount plus one for every resident. When it reaches the threshold, a raid is announced and the threat starts again at 0. The raid brings one raider and one more for every so many residents, and arrives a few minutes after the announcement at a chunk on the edge of your land. All the numbers are in the table above.

  • The target is the nearest watchtower that has had a guard, and without one the charter; a tower still waiting for its first guard is passed over.
  • A bigger settlement is raided more often and by more raiders. One of 12 residents sees its first raid a good three weeks after the grace period; one of 40 sees one every eight days.

How a raid is fought

A raid is fought in duels, one raider against one soldier, on several duel places at once. In each duel they strike in turn, blow for blow, the raider first; how often each hits and for how much is in the table above and on Soldiers and gear. Three duels run at once unless the server sets another number.

  • The soldiers of the target defend first, then those of every watchtower and barracks whose reach takes in the target, the nearest first.
  • Whoever loses a duel falls. A fallen soldier leaves the settlement, their post and bed come free, and the gear they wore is lost.
  • The winner stays on their place with their wounds and, after a short pause, faces the next opponent. Back in their own building a soldier heals slowly. A soldier fights in one raid at a time, and in none while they are away on an attack.

If every defender falls, the raiders plunder the store. Each raider still standing carries off a load of goods, the most valuable by the unit first — valued at what a village pays for them, emeralds one to one. A good no village pays for, like a torch, they never take. A settlement without a single defender is plundered as soon as the raiders arrive.

A raid on a village

A connected village has a threat of its own. It grows by one for every bed of the village on top of the fixed amount, its grace period runs from the day it was connected, and its raids bring one raider more for every few beds. It is defended by the soldiers of every watchtower and barracks whose reach takes in its gate.

  • Fought off, the raid raises your reputation with the village; lost, it lowers it, and for a few days the village sends neither people nor trade. The connection stays, and with it the protection of its land.
  • When the connection ends, its threat ends, and a raid already announced against the village is called off.

How you hear of it

Two lines in the chat for every raid, to everybody with a role in the settlement:

  • when it is announced: “X: 3 raiders are coming. Their target: Y.”, or for a village “X: 3 raiders are coming for a connected village.”
  • when it ends: “X fought off the raid. 1 soldier fell.”, “X lost the raid. 2 soldiers fell, and the raiders carried off …”, for a village “X fought off the raid on its village: reputation +20. …” or “X lost the raid on its village: reputation −20, and for 3 days it brings neither people nor trade. …”, and “X: the raid has been called off.” when it does not come.

A raid marker stands over the target from the announcement until the raid ends: a red label — “Raid: 3 raiders”, counting down as they fall — and a red beam about 48 blocks high that shows through ground and buildings, so you find the raid in a large settlement. Everybody with a role sees it; the place where the raiders arrive is not marked.

Each player switches both for themselves, on the overview of the charter screen: Raids in chat: on/off for the two lines and Raid markers: on/off for the marker, both on by default. M does not hide the raid marker — it hides only the messages over buildings.

The overview of the charter screen shows the threat of the settlement and of every connected village against the threshold, or how much grace is left; every raid under way with its target and when it arrives — “3 raiders are coming. Target: Watchtower 2, arriving in 5:00 min.” — or how the fight stands; and the last raid with how it ended.

What you see

If you stand near enough, a raid is a scene. But the outcome is settled before you see it: the figures show it, they do not decide it.

  • The raiders are figures of their own in the shape of a vindicator with an axe. They wait just outside your land and march across the boundary in time to reach the target. A raid on a village comes out of the village.
  • The soldiers who will fight gather at the target while the raiders come, and wait beside its door.
  • The duels are fought in front of the target, in the open. A hit flashes red, a miss glances off the shield. The fallen fall over and vanish. If the raiders have won, the rest of them walk away.
  • A soldier shows what they carry: the weapon in one hand, the shield in the other, the armour on the body.
  • Raiders attack no player, no villager and no block, they cannot be hurt, and their vanishing changes nothing.
