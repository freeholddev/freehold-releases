Boundary and protection

Your land is the claim of your charter — 7×7 chunks, see Founding a settlement — and of every watchtower you build.

Sealed land

Inside your boundary, nothing changes a block but you and your members. The land you have claimed is sealed: you may dig, build and break as you like, and nobody and nothing else may. A stranger who swings a pickaxe there is told the land is yours. A creeper that walks in blows up without taking a single block with it — one standing right on the border still leaves its crater on the outside. Withers, endermen and anything else that would help itself to your walls are refused the same way.

Buckets and pistons are held at the boundary as well. A stranger's bucket neither pours nor scoops on your land, and a piston outside your boundary pushes nothing in and pulls nothing out. Your own pistons work as they always do.

Three things get through, and they are worth knowing. Fire still spreads, flowing lava or water still moves, and a dispenser standing right at your boundary still pours or lights the one block in front of it. A fire lit outside your boundary burns its way in. What it takes of your buildings and roads the settlement puts back by itself; the rest of your land it does not.

Using what stands on your land

Only the freeholder and the members use anything on your land. A visitor and a player without a role open no chest there, flip no lever, sleep in no bed and take nothing out of an item frame or off an armour stand. Above the hotbar they read “This land belongs to X. Only its freeholder and members may use what stands on it.” What they hold in their hand still works — they can eat, for instance.

Doors and fence gates stay open to everybody, so nobody can be shut in on your land, and so does the anchor of a building, which shows a stranger the settlement's card.

A server can loosen two things: it can open chests and other containers to everybody, and it decides whether strangers may hand goods in at your goods ports — as the game starts, they may. See Game rules.

A connected village stays open for use. Its land is sealed against change, not against use — see Villages.

Lightning does no harm on your land. A bolt over your claim strikes for the eye and the ear only: it lights no fire and harms nobody standing there. Thunder still rolls, a lightning rod still draws the bolt, and copper still gets cleaned by it.

No monster spawns inside your boundary on its own. Night, a pillager patrol and a zombie calling for help bring nothing up on your land. A monster spawner, a spawn egg or a raid still works as it always does, and anything that spawned outside may still walk in.

The land of a village you have connected is sealed for you as well, with a few differences — the villagers still farm, and lightning still strikes there. See Villages.

Freehold's own raiders are something else: they come for your settlement whatever the seal, but they break nothing — see Raids.

An orchard's trees are protected wherever they stand, even outside your boundary — see Orchard.

March stones

The boundary stays visible once you have placed. A march stone goes up on every chunk corner along the edge of your land, following the ground up and down a hill. There is a gap wherever the edge falls on water or lava. Like any building block they cannot be broken, by you either, and one that goes missing is set again; taking one away would not shrink anything anyway, because the charter and your watchtowers are what hold the land.

Seeing the borders

While you hold N, the border of every settlement in sight lies on the ground as a line, block by block along the lie of the land, in that settlement's own colour. The line follows a claim that has grown through watchtowers, and it shows the hole a conquest has left in one.

  • The name of the settlement stands in its colour where its border comes nearest you, a few blocks on its side of the line — so two neighbours who share a border stand apart, each on its own side.
  • Over every building of every settlement, your own and foreign ones, stands its name and number in the same colour.
  • A settlement keeps its colour for every player and after every restart. Red, green and amber never stand for a settlement: they belong to placing, to the raid marker and to the messages over buildings.

The key can be moved in the controls menu, as Show building names and borders (hold) — see Controls.

What the settlement repairs

Your buildings, roads, crossing lanterns and march stones are put back by themselves, quietly and for nothing — no material, no builder, no message — wherever a player is near.

  • A building: a block that is missing, burning or flooded is set again, and a fire in the building is put out. What you have put into a building yourself — chests, furniture — is left alone.
  • A road: a stretch whose surface is missing or overgrown is laid anew, and a block set on the road is broken out and drops as an item. What is built higher up — a roof, a bridge — is left alone.
  • A crossing lantern or a march stone that is missing is set again.

Water and lava at a building

What water or lava washes out is put back only once the water or lava has gone. Torches, flowers, carpets and the like would be taken again the moment they were set, so the settlement leaves the gap until you take the water away; after that the block is back within about two minutes. Walls, floors, doors and other solid blocks are set again at once.

Only a building that stands in the current version of its drawing is repaired. A building raised from an older version — before an update of the mod, before its settlement changed its theme, or a conquered watchtower still standing in the theme of its old owner — stays as it stands and shows the hint “X was built to an older version of its drawing and is no longer repaired by itself. It can be rebuilt to the current drawing in the buildings tab.”

Lift it, and it is repaired again. On the buildings tab such a building has a button Lift to current drawing. It asks first, because every block of the building is set anew and whatever was placed inside it is lost. Lifting costs nothing and needs no builder; the building goes on working and keeps its level. The freeholder and members may lift. An upgrade does not do it: an older building is still older after it.

Neighbours

Your lumberjacks and foresters never work on another freehold's land, even where two boundaries touch. Land that belongs to nobody they work like their own.

  • Work does not stop at your border — but it stops at a neighbour and at a village. A lumberjack near the edge fells on nobody's land just as it would on yours. It never touches land another freehold has claimed, and it leaves a village alone: every chunk with a village bed in it, and one chunk all round, is not yours to clear or plant.
  • Another settlement takes your land in one way only: when it conquers one of your watchtowers, it takes the land only that tower held and the chunks it stands on — see Attacks.
  • Only those you give a role have a say. Players without a role in your settlement cannot build or tear down in it, they do not see its messages, and instead of its charter screen they see its card. Neither they nor a visitor use what stands on your land — see Using what stands on your land. You offer roles on the Members tab.
