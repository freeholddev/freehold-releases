Attacks

An attack is one settlement sending its soldiers against a watchtower of another — or against its charter, once that settlement has no watchtower left that has had a guard. A tower that falls changes hands with its land; a charter that falls ends its settlement. A server can switch attacks off — see Game rules.

Shieldno attack on a conquered watchtower for 1 day

Who may attack whom

The freeholder and the members of a settlement may attack out of it, and only a settlement in which they hold no role. A member of another settlement cannot attack it, not even out of their own. Settlements of the same freeholder never attack each other.

  • A watchtower can be attacked once its first guard has taken the post, and from then on even when the post stands empty. A site cannot, and neither can a finished tower that has never had a guard; it is not listed on the tab Attack.
  • The charter can be attacked only once no watchtower of its settlement stands that has had a guard. A tower still waiting for its first guard does not shield it.
  • A new settlement is safe for a grace period from its founding, and a conquered tower for a while after its conquest — both are in the table above. Raiders still come for a conquered tower; only attacks leave it alone.

Who goes

The soldiers of your watchtowers and barracks within reach of the target can go, and every watchtower keeps one. Reach is measured in a straight line from their building to the target, ignoring height. A barracks gives all its soldiers, a watchtower all but one — so a watchtower of level 1, with a single post, gives none, and an attack takes a barracks or a tower of level 2.

  • The soldiers of the building nearest the target go first.
  • Whoever is fighting a raid, or is already out on an attack, does not go.
  • A soldier who goes keeps their post, their bed, their gear and their wounds. Their post is not filled while they are away, their gear wears on, and they do not fight a raid on their own building until they are back. On the buildings tab they are marked (away on an attack).

Launching an attack

An attack is launched from an attack line, and there are two ways to it.

  • Right-click the anchor of a foreign watchtower with an empty hand. The card of its settlement opens, and under the tower stands one attack line for every settlement of yours out of which you may attack it. The same goes for a foreign charter, whose line stays grey while a watchtower of its settlement stands that has had a guard.
  • The tab Attack on your own charter screen lists every foreign watchtower your soldiers reach and that has had a guard — and a charter, once no such tower of its settlement stands — the nearest first. It reads “No foreign watchtower stands in reach of your watchtowers and barracks.” when there is none.

The line says who, from where, and how many can go — “Watchtower 2 of Y, from X: 3 soldiers can go, 64 blocks away.” — and under it are −, + and Attack with 1. One soldier is set to begin with. An attack once launched cannot be called back.

A line that cannot be used is grey and says why:

  • “Attacks between freeholds are switched off in this world.”
  • “Freeholds of the same owner never attack each other.”
  • “The charter can only be attacked once no watchtower of its freehold stands that has ever had a guard.”
  • “A new freehold: no attack on it for 2:40 h more.” — the grace period
  • “Conquered a short while ago: no attack on it for 45:10 min more.” — the shield after a conquest
  • “No soldier in reach can go: every watchtower keeps one, and nobody fights twice.”
  • “That many soldiers cannot go.”
  • “You hold a role in Y and cannot attack it.”
  • “Only the owner and the members of X may attack out of it.”

The march and the fight

The soldiers march to the target, and the fight begins when the farthest of them has arrived — never sooner than the shortest lead in the table above.

  • The fight is fought in duels, as in a raid, one attacker against one defender on each of several duel places at once, and the attacker strikes first. The winner stays on their place for the next opponent.
  • A watchtower is defended by its own soldiers, then by those of every watchtower and barracks of its settlement whose reach takes it in, the nearest first. The charter is defended by the barracks in reach.
  • Without a defender, the attack is won with the first blow.
  • Whoever falls is lost with their gear, on either side.

After the fight, the attackers return home, won or lost: each takes as long as they took to get there. On the way they do not heal and fight no raid. An attack plunders nothing — a lost one costs the attacker only their fallen.

A conquered watchtower

The attacker who wins takes the watchtower, and one soldier moves in. One of the attackers still standing takes a post in the tower and lives there, with their gear and their wounds; their old post comes free, and the settlement fills it, and any other post of the tower, like any other. The goods in the tower's stacks go to the store of the settlement that lost it. The tower takes the next watchtower number of the settlement that conquered it. Its blocks stay as they stand: if the conqueror builds in another theme, the tower counts as standing in an older version of its drawing until it is lifted.

The land that only the tower held goes with it. Land that the charter or another watchtower of the defender also holds stays with the defender — except the chunks the conquered tower stands on: those always go with the tower, even where the defender's charter or another of their towers held them too, and leave a hole in the defender's land. A conquered tower never stands on the land of its old owner. Only a chunk the defender's charter itself stands on stays theirs.

What the defender had standing on the land that changed hands is torn down, and nothing of it comes back.

  • Every building, site and upgrade on that land falls as if the defender had torn it down — but none of its cost is returned. Only what lay in its stacks and in its people's hands goes to their store. Those who worked there look for another post; those who lived there look for another bed, and move out if there is none.
  • A building that falls in a fight burns down. The flames are only a picture: nothing catches fire and nothing spreads.
  • Every road stretch that touches that land falls the same way, and so do the roads that ended at the conquered tower. What is left of a road on the defender's own land stays theirs.
  • A village gate on that land ends its connection.

The conqueror gets the land empty, and draws their own roads on it. The march stones of both settlements move to their new edges, and the villages near them are found again — see Boundary and protection.

The end of a settlement

When the charter falls, its settlement ends. Every building and road of it is torn down with nothing returned — the buildings burn down together, as above — its residents move out, its store is lost, every role in it lapses and every village connection ends. Raids and attacks on it end without a fight. Nobody moves in.

  • Its land falls to the settlements around it that had to leave it out, as when a watchtower is torn down.
  • It no longer counts against the number of settlements a server lets one player own.
  • Its players read “X has fallen with its charter and is no more.”

How you hear of it

Two lines in the chat for every attack, to everybody with a role in either settlement, as for a raid and with the same switch Raids in chat:

  • when it is launched: “X attacks the Watchtower 2 of Y with 3 soldiers.”
  • when it ends: “X took the Watchtower 2 of Y. 1 attacker fell, and 2 defenders.”, “Y fought off the attack of X. 3 attackers fell, and 2 defenders.”, “X took the charter of Y. Y is no more.”, or “The attack of X on Y has been called off; its soldiers return home.” when the server switches attacks off during the march.

The defender sees a marker over the target, like a raid marker — “Attack: 3 soldiers” — with the same switch Raid markers.

The overview of the charter screen shows every attack under way, one of yours or one against you, with its target and how long until the fight or how it stands — “X attacks our Watchtower 2 with 3 soldiers; the fight in 45 s.”, “Our attack on the Watchtower 2 of Y: 2 soldiers on the march, the fight in 1:10 min.” — and the last attack with how it ended. In its grace period it says “No attack can come for 6 more days.” On the buildings tab a conquered tower says how long its shield lasts: “No attack on it for 45:10 min more.”

What you see

If you stand near enough, an attack is a scene, but the outcome is settled before you see it: the figures show it, they do not decide it.

  • The attackers walk from their buildings to the target, one block a second, with their gear.
  • The defenders gather at the duel places in front of the target, as against a raid, and the first attackers march straight to theirs.
  • The duels show blows whose outcome is already fixed; whoever falls falls over.
  • After the fight the survivors return home.
