Villages

A village near your land can be connected to your settlement, and a connected village sends you people, trades with you and is protected like your own land. You connect it with a village gate on the edge of your land and a road from the settlement to that gate. A village that is only near brings nothing.

Finding a village

The villages near your boundary are found at the founding and again whenever your boundary moves (see the watchtower), and never in between. A village counts as near if it lies within 48 blocks of your boundary. The overview of the charter screen lists each one with its beds and its distance, and says not connected until it is.

It has to be a village the world generated, and its size is taken as you first find it. Beds you put down yourself are not a village, and beds you add to a real village do not enlarge it. A village you have never been near is not found: walk there once, then found or move your boundary.

You may not found on top of one. If a village would fall inside your claim the placement stays red and tells you how many blocks to move. The place you are looking for is outside your boundary and within those 48 blocks — close enough to reach with a gate, far enough to stay its own place. A watchtower may claim up to a village, but never the village's own land: every chunk with one of its beds in it, and one chunk all round.

Connecting a village

A village is connected while its gate stands, the gate's link road into the village is finished, and one of your finished roads ends at the gate. How to place the gate and what its messages mean is on Village Gate.

  • A village is connected to one settlement at most — whoever comes first. Another can connect it only once the first loses the connection.
  • When the connection is found or lost, the chat says so: “X is now connected to 1 village with 8 beds. People move in from there.” or “X is connected to no village any more.”

What a connected village brings

  • People. Every bed of a connected village adds to the newcomers your settlement draws on its own — the exact share is on the charter's page, and it grows with your reputation there. See Residents.
  • Trade. Your trade posts sell to it and buy from it, for emeralds. A village only turns over so many emeralds a day, one for each of its beds.
  • Protection. Its land is sealed for your settlement, see below.
  • A threat of its own. Raiders come for a connected village too, and your soldiers defend it — see Raids.

Reputation

Your settlement has a reputation with every village it has connected, and it is what trade earns you. Every emerald your trade posts turn over with the village raises it, it sinks a little every day, and it has a ceiling. The higher it is, the more each of the village's beds counts for your newcomers — up to twice as much. A raid on the village fought off raises it, one lost lowers it. The villagers' own gossip plays no part. When the connection ends the reputation stays as it is and goes on sinking. The numbers are on the charter's page.

A connected village is protected

The land of a connected village is sealed like your own claim — the chunks with its beds and one chunk all round. Only the freeholder and the members of the connecting settlement may break or set blocks there; anybody else is told “This village is connected to X. Only its freeholder and members may change its land.” Explosions and block-breaking mobs do nothing there. Using what stands there stays open to everybody — the villagers' beds, chests and bell, unlike in your claim (see Using what stands on your land).

  • The villagers still work their fields: their own harvesting and sowing goes on.
  • No monster spawns there on its own — not at night, not with a patrol, not as a zombie's reinforcement — as in your claim.
  • No vanilla raid starts at a connected village. A player with Bad Omen gets no Raid Omen there and keeps the Bad Omen.
  • Lightning still strikes as in vanilla; only your claim is spared it.
  • The protection ends with the connection.
